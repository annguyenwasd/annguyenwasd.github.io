4K upscale

Increase the sharpness and detail of an old photo while preserving identity, composition, and lighting.

English prompt
Ultra-high-resolution 4K upscale, adhering strictly to the provided reference image. Absolute fidelity to the facial structure, proportions, and identity of the original. Preserve the expression, gaze, pose, camera angle, framing, and perspective exactly, with no drift. Clothing, hair, skin, and all background details must keep their original structure, placement, and design. Recover fine detail with natural realism. Clarify pores, fine wrinkles, individual hair strands, eyelashes, fabric fibers, seams, and material edges without stylization. Keep the original colors, white balance, and tonal relationships exactly as captured. Light direction, intensity, contrast, and shadow falloff must match the original precisely — only sharper, with a wider dynamic range. Do not change the lighting, do not alter body shape. Remove noise. Apply controlled sharpening and reconstruct high-frequency detail, removing compression artifacts and noise while keeping believable texture. No smoothing, no plastic skin, no artificial gloss. Facial features must stay consistent across the whole image, with plausible anatomy, clean contours, and stability. Exclusion constraints: no warping, no off-focus facial shifts, no added or missing body parts, no editing of hands, no deformation, no perspective change, no inserted text or graphics, no invented detail, no stylization. The result must be a photographic, true-to-reference upscale — matching the reference image exactly, only clearer, crisper, and higher resolution.

03

Double-chin fix

Gently correct the distorted perspective caused by an unflattering low camera angle.

English prompt
Apply a subtle refinement to the unflattering camera angle in the uploaded photo while fully preserving the face, features, and identity of the subject and the entire scene. If the low shooting angle exaggerates the chin or jawline, adjust the perspective slightly to simulate a slightly higher, more flattering angle. Reduce the double chin or the excess shadow under the chin caused by the bad angle — not by removing anatomy, but by simulating better lighting and a more flattering viewpoint. Define the jawline slightly so it looks slimmer and natural, the way the subject looks in real life from eye level. If the nostrils are prominent because of the low angle, reduce their prominence slightly toward a natural frontal view. Adjust neck length and posture subtly if the angle compressed them unnaturally. Every edit must be extremely subtle and natural; the subject must still look exactly like themselves, just photographed from a nicer angle. Do not change any facial feature, do not slim the face, do not alter bone structure. Only correct the distortion caused by the poor camera angle. The final result must look exactly like the same photo taken from a slightly nicer, eye-level position.

English prompt

Match each person's eye shape, eyelid position, gaze direction, and lighting. Do not change facial structure, expression, skin texture, hairstyle, head position, or camera angle. Do not borrow eyes from other people, do not exaggerate eye size or brightness. The result must look like a real photograph taken at the same moment when everyone had their eyes open.

07

Restore old photos

Bring back blurred, scratched, torn, or faded photos with the feel of their era.

English prompt
Restore this photograph with era-appropriate technique, addressing every age-related problem such as blur, damage, fading, scratches, tears, creases, worn areas, or black-and-white media. First analyze the image to determine its approximate period and the original photographic process, ensuring the restoration is historically accurate. Make the image fresh and clear by gently sharpening soft edges and facial features without overdoing it, smoothing grain or noise if present, and reconstructing missing parts with realistic texture that matches the original. If the color has faded or never existed, bring it back naturally and vividly but true to the photography technology of the period, without looking artificial; balance the color for natural light, adjust brightness and contrast so everything stands out, and preserve the original tonal values. Add subtle detail to faces, objects, or background elements that may have been lost, such as fabric weave on clothing, lifelike skin texture, or small details in the scene, while keeping the overall sense of realism, preserving natural film grain, and not changing the composition. Make sure the whole image is balanced, with no harsh shadows or blown highlights, removing technical flaws while still respecting the nostalgic feel and the era's characteristic exposure. Finally, upscale to a higher resolution such as Full HD 32k for extra clarity, and export in a photorealistic style, looking like a professionally restored photo or a recently taken high-quality image.
